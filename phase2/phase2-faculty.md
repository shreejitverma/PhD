## Cross-cutting conclusions
1. Every batch independently converged on the same binding constraint: zero publications. The thesis-to-arXiv conversion helps all 12 targets simultaneously. Two framings from one artifact: quantitative (LOB measurement/execution results — for Pelger/Mastrolia/Capponi/Moallemi/Leung) and systems (input-dependent stream processing with market data as workload — for Hoe/Stellato).
2. The second recurring gap is measure-theoretic probability. It is disqualifying at CMU Math and Princeton's math-finance core, material at Columbia/Berkeley theory groups, and irrelevant at Stevens/CMU ECE. A graduate real-analysis or measure-theoretic probability course (or documented self-study + GRE Math Subject) before December materially helps five applications.
3. GRE plan confirmed: required at Stanford MS&E, Berkeley IEOR, Booth, Stevens FE; optional at MIT ORC, UW, CMU ECE, Princeton (F26 policy). One sitting by early October covers all.
4. No professor in the entire US mapping holds an NSF award ending after 2029 except Wenpin Tang (2031) and Sriraman (Feb 2029) and Xin Guo (Aug 2029). Funding decisions therefore ride on departments/centers, not PI grants — cohort-funded programs (Princeton, Columbia, MIT, Stanford, Booth) carry the least funding risk; Stevens carries the most.
